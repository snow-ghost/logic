# The traditional laws of thought

These rules concern the form and justification of reasoning, not the choice of
subject matter. The sufficient-reason principle is presented here as part of
Chelpanov's traditional framework, not as a theorem of modern predicate logic.

## 1. Identity

**Formula:** A is A.

Keep a concept's meaning constant during an argument. An unnoticed change of
meaning creates equivocation (*equivocatio*) and can turn a three-term syllogism
into four terms (*quaternio terminorum*).

Example: after stating the composition of table salt, a writer uses “salt” to
mean any chemical salt. The wider class cannot inherit every property of the
original substance.

## 2. Non-contradiction

**Formula:** A cannot be both B and not-B at the same time and in the same respect.

Keep qualifications explicit. A surface can be white in one region and not white
in another without contradiction. Likewise, a state can change over time.

In a proof by contradiction (*reductio ad absurdum*), show that an assumption,
together with the accepted premises, entails incompatible conclusions. Check
which assumption is responsible before claiming it has been refuted.

## 3. Excluded middle

**Formula:** A or not-A (*tertium non datur*).

One of a pair of contradictories must be true in classical logic. “Conifer” and
“non-conifer” exhaust the relevant domain; “conifer” and “oak” do not.

This supports reasoning by elimination when the alternatives have actually been
shown to exhaust the possibilities. It does not justify an arbitrary two-option
choice or a false dilemma.

## 4. Sufficient reason

**Formula:** an assertion requires sufficient grounds (*lex rationis sufficientis*).

Ask what justifies accepting the assertion, rather than treating confidence or
repetition as evidence.

- **Ratio cognoscendi:** a reason for knowing or believing a claim.
- **Ratio fiendi:** the real cause of an event.

The rise of a thermometer can be evidence that a room warmed. A heater may be
the cause of that warming. Evidence and cause play different roles.

## Examples

“A bat is a mammal; this baseball bat is a bat; therefore it is a mammal” shifts
the meaning of the middle term and violates identity.

“All people are rational” and “Some people are not rational,” with the same
meaning and qualifications, are A and O contradictories. They cannot both be true.

An ordinary Euclidean triangle is acute, right, or obtuse. If it is neither
acute nor obtuse, it is right. The elimination works because the alternatives
are exhaustive and exclusive in the stated domain.

“The stock will rise tomorrow because I feel it will” supplies no adequate
evidence for the forecast. The issue is its grounds, not the mere possibility
that the forecast happens to be correct.
